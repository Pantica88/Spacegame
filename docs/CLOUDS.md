# Cloud rendering

Volumetric clouds for every planet with air, rebuilt from scratch on
2026-10-14. The painted weather maps, the editor's **Clouds** tab and saved
planets are unchanged: they are the renderer's input
([cloud painting](CLOUD_PAINTING.md)).

## Formations

Each body's weather map holds four formation weights per direction: R
cumulus, G stratocumulus, B overcast (light paint gives thin veils, which is
how the wispy tiles render), A storms and mushroom clouds. Mixed paint mixes
the formations. Bodies without a painted map get procedural weather: broad
patches of cumulus and stratocumulus sized by the **Procedural coverage**
slider.

| Paint | Shape in the sky |
| --- | --- |
| Cumulus | Separate heaps on a flat base. Heavier paint makes more and taller heaps, but always leaves clear sky between them. Crowns round off as coverage thins with height. |
| Stratocumulus | A layer of joined cells, each domed to its own height. Even full paint leaves dark seams and some holes; it never closes into a featureless sheet. |
| Overcast | A closed, slightly lumpy sheet low in the layer. Light paint gives a thin veil. |
| Storm | Below the cap band only heavy paint rises, as towers; in the cap band light paint spreads too, into an anvil. The band starts lower and reaches higher where paint is heavy. |
| Mushroom | The same rule: a round stamp's heavy centre rises as a narrow stem, its lighter ring spreads into a domed cap with a rounded underside. |

Storm paint up to 0.16 makes no cloud, so the faint background many storm
stamps leave behind does not haze the whole planet. Painted weights vary by
up to a fifth over about 60 km, so a broad stamp breaks into denser and
thinner patches. **Wind** drifts the whole map around the planet,
**Billow evolution** slowly moves the noise through it, and **Cloud density**
scales how opaque the cloud is.

## Layers

Heights scale with the atmosphere (`CloudLayer.heights()`), so thin
atmospheres get shallow cloud:

| | Formula | Verdant (90 km air, 2.8 km relief) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloud base | 3% of the air, at least half the relief, 0.6 to 6 km | 2.7 km |
| Top of the low formations | base + 2.5% of the air (0.6 to 3 km) | 4.95 km |
| Storm tops | base + 13% of the air (2 to 13 km), at most 60% of the air | 14.4 km |

Mountains above the base reach into the clouds. The editor's brush paints at
the low formations' mid-height.

## How it is drawn

Files: `assets/shaders/cloud_field.gdshaderinc` (the field: weather, density
and light), `scripts/cloud_render_pass.gd` (GPU passes),
`assets/shaders/cloud_composite.gdshader` (composite),
`scripts/cloud_layer.gd` (the node), `scripts/cloud_field.gd` (CPU port and
noise volumes).

1. **Field.** One include defines where cloud is and how it is lit. It is
   written in the subset of GLSL that both Godot shaders and Vulkan compute
   shaders accept, so the march, the ocean and the parity test share it word
   for word; `cloud_field.gd` ports it to GDScript. Positions are in
   kilometres in the cloud frame: planet-centred, turning with the planet and
   the wind, so the weather and the noise are fixed in it.
2. **Noise.** `assets/clouds/shape_noise.png` (64³: Perlin-Worley, Worley
   lumps, two broad Perlin channels) and `detail_noise.png` (32³ Worley and
   Perlin) are tileable volumes stored as stacked slices, generated by
   `tools/build_cloud_noise.py` (deterministic; pure Python). The shape volume
   repeats every 14 km, bent by a smooth warp so the repeat never lines up
   into a grid, and varies 55% as fast with height as across, so cells
   stand as columns and gaps go right through a layer. Storm towers use their
   own unstretched lookup so they billow evenly. Detail erosion (1.8 km
   repeat) makes torn, wispy bases and billowing tops.
3. **March.** A `CompositorEffect` on the viewing camera runs a compute
   shader before the transparent pass, at half the internal resolution. Each
   ray starts at the cloud shell, stops at the nearest scene geometry among
   the four pixels its texel covers, and skips the clear air below the base.
   Steps grow with distance and are bounded by the path length. The minimum
   step is capped at 60 m so a long horizon ray still resolves nearby billows.
   Per-pixel hashed seeds with a golden-ratio temporal sequence avoid coherent
   diagonal sampling patterns. In the low
   formations no step may climb more than a tenth of their depth, so thin
   sheets are never stepped over. Where the weather allows no cloud the ray
   jumps 3 km across the map at a time, or straight down to the low
   formations. Stepping from clear air into cloud backs up and approaches
   again finely, and inside cloud each step's optical depth stays near one;
   both keep the jittered samples from flickering between clear and opaque.
   Far away, a mip of the shape volume and softer formation edges stand in
   for cells smaller than a pixel.
4. **Light.** Four taps toward the sun estimate the cloud's own shadow. Three
   octaves of weaker, broader scattering stand in for multiple scattering, so
   thick cloud stays bright inside; a two-lobe phase gives silver linings. Sun
   colour comes from the air along the sun's path (and an evening tint
   matching the sky's), so cloud tops stay lit after the ground's sunset and
   undersides glow from below. The planet's shadow and a faint blue night sky
   light finish it.
5. **Resolve.** A second compute pass blends the fresh frame with the last
   one, reprojected through the cloud's mean depth (camera travel is computed
   in double precision on the CPU) and clamped to the fresh neighbourhood. It
   averages the march's per-frame jitter away and keeps history through
   ordinary flight; a cut, a jump or a skipped frame starts over.
6. **Composite.** A screen triangle in the transparent pass (render priority
   1: after the sun and the ocean, before exhaust and heat) upsamples the
   result. Each of the four nearest texels counts only if its march stopped
   at this pixel's geometry, or all its cloud lies in front of or behind it,
   so craft and terrain edges stay sharp. The haze between camera and cloud
   is the atmosphere's own scattering, using the sky's sunlight table.

The ocean includes the same field and mirrors the clouds with five coarse
samples along the reflected ray. Vessels get their sunlight shading
(`sun_transmittance()`) and surrounding cloud glow (`cloud_glow()`) from the
CPU port.

Without a `RenderingDevice` (the Compatibility renderer, headless runs) no
clouds are drawn; vessel lighting and the editor's data still work.

## Performance

Measured 2026-10-14 with `tests/benchmark_planet_rendering.gd`
(`BENCH_VARIANTS=full,no_clouds`, 1920×1080, two interleaved rounds of 90
frames) on the RX 580, Godot 4.7.2 double build, with the owner's saved
Verdant weather. *Cost* is the frame's GPU time with clouds minus without;
*passes* is the march and resolve alone, from their GPU timestamps (the rest
is the full-resolution composite and its haze).

| Scenario | Cost | Passes | Previous renderer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Surface, daylight | 1.33 ms | 0.96 ms | 4.0 ms |
| Surface, sunset | 1.93 ms | 1.53 ms | — |
| Ascent, 12 km, deck below | 3.22 ms | 2.63 ms | 6.9 ms |
| Upper atmosphere, 60 km | 2.59 ms | 1.77 ms | 8.0 ms |
| Low orbit, 150 km | 2.60 ms | 1.83 ms | 5.9 ms |
| High orbit, 1,600 km | 1.43 ms | 1.01 ms | 4.7 ms |
| Night side, 300 km | 1.46 ms | 1.04 ms | 4.0 ms |

The previous renderer's column is the same benchmark's attribution in
[RENDERING_PERFORMANCE.md](RENDERING_PERFORMANCE.md) (Godot 4.5.1, before
that report's atmosphere work). Its later tuned version measured about
3.7 ms near the ground and 2.7 ms at 100 km with a different benchmark
([roadmap](MULTI_PLANET_ROADMAP.md)). Costs grow with how much of the screen
looks into cloud: a view filling the screen with deck from 12 km measured
2.8 ms for the passes in `tests/_probe/cloud_cost.gd`. The march's work per
ray is visible in game with F9.

## Checks

```bash
./tools/run.sh --headless --script res://tests/test_clouds.gd
./tools/run.sh --resolution 1280x720 --script res://tests/test_clouds.gd
./tools/run.sh --headless --script res://tests/test_cloud_vessel_light.gd
```

`test_clouds.gd` checks, without a GPU: seamless noise, layer heights,
visibility rules (empty, cleared and procedural weather, disabled, airless),
the formation shapes (overcast closes, heavy stratocumulus keeps seams,
cumulus thicken with paint but keep gaps, faint storm paint stays clear, a
mushroom's cap spreads well beyond its stem, nothing passes the storm tops)
and wind drift. With a GPU it also runs the include in a compute shader at
512 points and compares weather, density and sunlight with the GDScript
port, then flies the production world view: clouds overhead must change the
image and stay finite, a wall 40 m ahead must stay in front of them, and
frames in motion must match the same pose held still. The windowed run
prints the measured differences.

`test_cloud_vessel_light.gd` covers vessel shading and glow;
`test_cloud_painting.gd`, `test_cloud_editor.gd`,
`test_cloud_save_persistence.gd` and `test_cloud_occupancy.gd` cover the
weather data; `test_flame_cloud_overlap.gd` the draw order against exhaust
and heat. All run in `tools/check.sh`.

Probes for looking and measuring (isolated `XDG_DATA_HOME` recommended):

| Probe | Use |
| --- | --- |
| `tests/_probe/cloud_views.gd` | Captures named views (pad, low flight, in and above the deck, 30 km, orbit, far) for saved, procedural, generated or ring-stamped weather, at any sun height. |
| `tests/_probe/cloud_motion.gd` | Captures a rocket-speed climb, fast flight under and over the deck and a quick turn, each frame paired with the same pose held still. |
| `tests/_probe/cloud_cost.gd` | Median GPU time of the cloud passes and of the frame, per view, with and without clouds. |
| `tests/_probe/cloud_targets.gd` | Reads the march's targets back and prints their statistics or a column of texels. |

In game, F9 cycles the cost view: the march colours each ray by its
density evaluations, and the overlay shows the cloud passes' GPU time.

## Limits

Clouds do not shadow the terrain and do not simulate weather; painting is
visual only. Overcast and veils share one weight, so wispy paint renders as a
low veil rather than high cirrus. The march re-reads the weather every half
kilometre along a ray, far finer than the map's 7 km texels, so this only
softens painted edges imperceptibly.

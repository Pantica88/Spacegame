4. How to capture a planet

I would use a multi-stage takeover system.

    Stage 1 — Establish presence

    Send exploration probes, construct a small station, and secure an orbital foothold.

    Stage 2 — Challenge orbital control

    Disable or defeat defending fleets, defensive satellites, and station-mounted weapons.

    Stage 3 — Isolate the command station

    Block supply routes, prevent reinforcements, and reduce the station's operational effectiveness.

    Stage 4 — Capture or negotiate surrender

    Deploy a boarding vessel to take control of the command station, or force a negotiated transfer of authority.

    Stage 5 — Consolidate control

    Repair the station, restore supplies, establish governance, and prevent the former owner from reclaiming it.

This is better than simply attacking a planet until its health reaches zero.

You could win a battle but still fail to capture the planet because your fleet runs out of fuel or the enemy receives reinforcements.
5. The important distinction: Orbit vs. surface

I would give each planet two separate control states.

Orbital Control

Determines who controls spacecraft traffic, orbital infrastructure, and the ability to blockade or reinforce the planet.

Surface Control

Determines who operates colonies, mining facilities, surface bases, and local infrastructure.

For an uninhabited planet, establishing an orbital command station and a surface outpost may be enough to claim it.

For an inhabited planet, controlling orbit alone shouldn't automatically grant political control. You would need treaties, settlements, or a much more costly occupation.

That also creates a useful distinction between claiming uninhabited worlds and conquering inhabited ones.

1. The core vision

Verdant begins as a realistic rocket-building and exploration game. Over time, it evolves into a civilization strategy game featuring planetary discovery, colonization, space infrastructure, AI-controlled civilizations, diplomacy, and military conflict.

The player starts with a simple objective: investigate an unidentified object that crashes on Verdant's moon.

As technology develops, the player discovers other planets, establishes colonies, constructs orbital stations, and eventually encounters intelligent alien civilizations.

The ultimate objective is no longer just reaching space. It becomes developing, expanding, and protecting a civilization across the solar system.
Core design principles

    Single-player first, with AI-controlled factions instead of multiplayer.

    Preserve KSP-style rocket construction, realistic flight physics, and orbital mechanics.

    Hide unexplored planets until scientific technology reveals them.

    Progress through discoveries, engineering achievements, and infrastructure rather than arbitrary science points.

    Introduce strategy and combat gradually.

    Ensure that rockets, spacecraft, planetary bases, and orbital stations remain interconnected.

    Use dynamic AI civilizations to create ongoing challenges after the main storyline is complete.

Eventually, station modules may also be manufactured in orbit.
6.1 Research Station

Primary responsibilities:

    Astronomical observation.

    Planetary scanning.

    Scientific experiments.

    Analysis of recovered samples.

    Alien technology research.

    Development of advanced components.

    Specialized scientific missions.

Potential modules:

    Research laboratory.

    Telescope array.

    Communications array.

    Scientific storage.

    Sensor systems.

    Experimental engineering laboratory.

Research efficiency depends on technological capability, assigned workforce, energy, equipment, and potentially orbital location.
6.2 Defence Station

Primary responsibilities:

    Defend orbital infrastructure.

    Detect hostile spacecraft.

    Coordinate defensive operations.

    Deploy and recover fighters.

    Protect transportation routes.

    Maintain local orbital security.

Potential modules:

    Fighter hangars.

    Weapons platforms.

    Radar and tracking systems.

    Tactical command center.

    Defensive armor.

    Missile or interceptor systems.

    Repair facilities.

Defence stations should be important military assets rather than arbitrary sources of territorial ownership.
6.3 Manufacturing Station

Primary responsibilities:

    Manufacture spacecraft.

    Produce station components.

    Process raw materials.

    Assemble satellites and fighters.

    Repair spacecraft.

    Store components and resources.

    Support fleet production.

Potential modules:

    Assembly facilities.

    Robotic construction systems.

    Manufacturing bays.

    Resource processing facilities.

    Storage containers.

    Spacecraft docking bays.

    Maintenance facilities.

Blueprint Production

The player designs a spacecraft manually.

After the design is saved and validated, manufacturing stations can produce additional copies.

Manufacturing should require:

    Required resources.

    Appropriate manufacturing technology.

    Sufficient station capacity.

    Electrical power.

    Workforce or automation capacity.

    Production time.

Resource requirements should depend on the components in the spacecraft blueprint.
6.4 Station Specialization

Stations have a primary role but may contain limited modules associated with other station types.

For example:

A manufacturing station may have a small defensive hangar but should not provide the same military capability as a dedicated defence station.
7. PLANETARY BASE SYSTEM

Players must be able to establish permanent bases on discovered planets and moons.

Planetary bases provide population, resources, industry, and territorial presence.
7.1 Base Modules

Potential structures include:

    Habitation buildings.

    Power generators.

    Food production.

    Water extraction.

    Oxygen and life support.

    Resource storage.

    Mining facilities.

    Surface manufacturing.

    Research facilities.

    Landing pads.

    Transport infrastructure.

    Defensive structures.

    Colony administration.

Base construction should consume resources.

Environmental conditions should affect construction requirements and operating costs.

For example, a breathable atmosphere may reduce life-support costs compared with an airless moon.
7.2 Population

Population is a strategic resource.

Each colony tracks:

    Current population.

    Maximum supported population.

    Housing capacity.

    Food supply.

    Water availability.

    Life-support capacity.

    Population growth.

    Available workforce.

    Assigned workforce.

    Colony stability.

Population should grow when infrastructure and living conditions support it.

Moving colonists between planets requires transportation capability and sufficient destination infrastructure.
7.3 Workforce Allocation

Population supports the operation of:

    Research stations.

    Manufacturing stations.

    Defence stations.

    Planetary industries.

    Mining operations.

    Administrative facilities.

Automation technology can reduce staffing requirements.

Population must not grow without constraints. Housing, food, life support, and available infrastructure should limit expansion.

For performance, population should initially be represented through aggregate numbers instead of individually simulated NPCs.
8. RESOURCE ECONOMY AND LOGISTICS

Planetary and orbital infrastructure should require resources to construct and operate.

Start with a small, understandable resource system.

Possible initial resources:

    Structural materials.

    Industrial metals.

    Fuel.

    Electronics.

    Energy.

    Food and life-support supplies.

Rare resources may be introduced later to support advanced technologies.

Resources may be obtained from:

    Verdant.

    Planetary mines.

    Moon mining outposts.

    Asteroids.

    Trading with AI civilizations.

Resources must be transported between locations.

Initially, the player manually designs and operates supply rockets.

Once reliable spacecraft and routes have been established, repeatable transportation missions may become automated.

The objective is to make spacecraft engineering relevant to civilization management.
9. SPACECRAFT MANUFACTURING

Orbital manufacturing stations should eventually produce spacecraft without launching them from a planetary surface.

Supported spacecraft categories:

    Scientific satellites.

    Reconnaissance probes.

    Cargo transports.

    Mining ships.

    Repair spacecraft.

    Interceptors.

    Heavy fighters.

    Defensive drones.

    Larger military vessels in later progression.

Manufactured ships should inherit their functional characteristics from their saved designs.

For example:

    Mass.

    Fuel capacity.

    Thrust.

    Maneuverability.

    Electrical capacity.

    Weapon systems.

    Sensor capabilities.

    Armor.

    Cargo capacity.

Do not use arbitrary spacecraft strength values as a replacement for the engineering system.

Simplified strategic statistics may be calculated from real component properties for AI simulation and performance optimization.
10. TACTICAL COMBAT

Combat should become available gradually, not immediately at the beginning of the campaign.
10.1 First Combat Encounter

Introduce an automated defensive drone discovered near the lunar crash site.

The encounter should support alternative solutions:

    Observe the drone.

    Avoid detection.

    Disable it.

    Destroy it.

    Investigate its behavior.

This serves as an introduction to tactical gameplay.
10.2 Space Combat

Players should eventually construct military spacecraft and organize fleets.

Combat mechanics may include:

    Weapon range.

    Target detection.

    Shielding or armor.

    Structural damage.

    Power consumption.

    Heat management.

    Fuel consumption.

    Thruster capability.

    Maneuvering.

    Defensive systems.

    Fighter deployment.

    Fleet commands.

10.3 AI-Controlled Fighters

Fighters should support orders such as:

    Patrol.

    Defend station.

    Intercept target.

    Escort spacecraft.

    Attack selected target.

    Retreat.

    Return for refueling.

    Return for repairs.

The player should be able to personally pilot a spacecraft when desired.
10.4 Physics and Performance

Nearby player-controlled spacecraft should use the existing detailed flight simulation where practical.

Distant AI battles may use a simplified tactical simulation derived from spacecraft capabilities.

This is necessary to avoid excessive processing costs when many spacecraft are active.

Combat should emphasize strategy and engineering rather than requiring fast reflexes.
11. PLANETARY OWNERSHIP AND CONTROL

A planet should not become owned automatically when discovered.

Territorial ownership must depend on infrastructure, recognized claims, and the ability to maintain control.
11.1 Orbital Control

Orbital control depends on:

    Operational defence stations.

    Fleet presence.

    Command infrastructure.

    Ability to maintain supply routes.

    Access to important orbital regions.

11.2 Surface Control

Surface control depends on:

    Permanent planetary bases.

    Colony infrastructure.

    Population.

    Planetary administration.

    Available security.

    Political agreements or territorial control.

11.3 Planetary Command Core

Implement a special command module that can be installed on an existing orbital station.

Its responsibilities include:

    Establishing administrative authority.

    Coordinating planetary infrastructure.

    Managing territorial claims.

    Tracking local strategic assets.

    Supporting planetary defence.

    Coordinating resource and logistics systems.

A functioning command core does not automatically guarantee total planetary ownership.
11.4 Planetary Influence

Develop a planetary influence system that considers:

    Command infrastructure.

    Military presence.

    Population and settlements.

    Manufacturing infrastructure.

    Research infrastructure.

    Supply reliability.

    Political relationships.

Influence should be distinct from official ownership.

Influence can be displayed as a numerical score, but actual ownership conditions should be clearly defined.
11.5 Planetary Takeover

A planetary takeover may involve:

    Establishing presence near the planet.

    Securing orbital access.

    Defeating or bypassing orbital defences.

    Disrupting enemy supply routes.

    Capturing or negotiating control of command infrastructure.

    Securing necessary surface infrastructure or political agreements.

    Maintaining supply lines and governance after takeover.

For uninhabited planets, establishment of a permanent base and command infrastructure may be enough to claim territory.

Inhabited planets require diplomatic, political, or military resolution.

Conquered populations should not automatically behave as loyal workers.

    Spacecraft designs.

    Infrastructure.

    Military strength.

    Diplomatic relationships.

    Strategic objectives.

    Discover planets.

    Manufacture spacecraft.

    Build defensive fleets.

    Claim territory.

    Negotiate agreements.

    Trade resources.

    Enter conflicts.

12.1 AI Personalities

Possible faction archetypes:

Scientific Civilization

Prioritizes exploration, scientific advancement, and peaceful cooperation.

Industrial Civilization

Prioritizes resource extraction, manufacturing, economic expansion, and trade.

Militaristic Civilization

Prioritizes defence, fleet construction, and territorial control.

Diplomatic Civilization

Prioritizes alliances, negotiations, population growth, and political influence.

Faction behavior should be influenced by available resources, strategic objectives, and relationships rather than fixed scripted actions alone.

14. PROGRESSION AND LONG-TERM GAMEPLAY

The game must not reach a dead end when all planets have been discovered.

Exploration should transition naturally into civilization strategy.

Core recurring gameplay loop:

    Discover planets, resources, and strategic opportunities.

    Develop technologies and spacecraft.

    Construct orbital and planetary infrastructure.

    Grow population and industrial capacity.

    Manufacture spacecraft and establish logistics networks.

    Protect infrastructure and compete with other civilizations.

    Negotiate, trade, expand, or capture territory.

    Respond to changing strategic conditions.

    Continue developing civilization.

Possible major victory conditions:

    Establish a dominant interplanetary civilization.

    Unify multiple civilizations through diplomacy.

    Achieve technological supremacy.

    Establish a self-sufficient solar-system economy.

    Complete an interstellar exploration mission.

    Resolve the central mystery surrounding the crashed spacecraft.

Players should be able to continue playing after completing a primary victory condition.

17. FIRST PLAYABLE VERTICAL SLICE

Before building the entire strategy game, produce a small but complete version connecting the major systems.

Required scenario:

    The player begins on Verdant.

    A mysterious object has crashed on the moon.

    The player designs and launches a scientific spacecraft.

    Scientific observations reveal additional information.

    The player unlocks and launches a basic orbital station module.

    The player constructs a functional research station.

    The research station provides access to a new spacecraft component or discovery.

    The player constructs a basic manufacturing facility in orbit.

    The manufacturing facility produces an unmanned fighter or utility drone from a saved blueprint.

    The player uses the spacecraft to complete an encounter involving an automated alien drone.

This vertical slice should prove that engineering, exploration, research, manufacturing, and strategic gameplay work together.

Only expand the scope once the integrated gameplay loop is functioning.

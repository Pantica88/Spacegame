# Orbital map

Map mode disables the main viewport's 3D pass and suspends hidden craft, flame, camera, and atmosphere updates; physics and the solar clock continue. Closing the map synchronizes flight geometry to current telemetry before drawing it again. The map uses 12 atmosphere view samples and 3 sunlight samples, while flight retains 64 and 6. Trajectory meshes and materials are reused.

`tests/benchmark_map.gd` measures 240 orbiting frames after a 60-frame warm-up, with vsync disabled, at the project viewport size. On the development RX 580, the zoomed local-map test went from 7.534 to 3.991 ms/frame after these changes. This isolated rendering benchmark is not a guarantee of whole-game FPS under time warp or on other hardware.

Press M during flight for the 3D map. Right-drag rotates the camera, and the wheel zooms. The Local orbit view renders the same globe as flight with a depth-tested predicted coast path, craft marker, and valid above-surface apoapsis/periapsis markers. Prediction excludes thrust and atmospheric drag. Apsides below the surface are omitted. Solar System mode reads editable planet resources from `assets/planets/*.tres`; each resource controls its map color, orbit visibility, distance, period and phase.

Amber shows recorded past flight; blue shows a bound orbit, coral shows predicted impact, and gold shows escape. The mint craft icon follows the projected trajectory; clicking it focuses the camera on the craft. Impact predictions end at a surface crosshair. Outbound escape paths do not mark past surface crossings as future impacts. Above-surface apsides remain visible, except an apoapsis already passed on an impact descent. The compact legend, current prediction status, and dark label backplates keep the map readable over terrain. History records while the map is closed and resets on rollout. Samples initially capture at quarter-second simulation intervals; long flights progressively simplify older detail to stay below 4096 points while retaining the launch point. A live end segment joins the trail to the craft. Recorded history is session-only and follows inertial flight coordinates, rather than rotating with the surface. The path and markers update together when telemetry changes.

Solar System switches to an overview of the Sun and every planet resource with orbit display enabled. Click a planet in the left list to focus it; Sun restores the overview. Orbital distances are linear in AU. Orbits are circular and coplanar; initial phases are illustrative, not ephemerides for a particular date. Body sizes are enlarged for visibility.

The local coast and maneuver predictors use the home planet's configured radius and gravity. The Solar System overview does not replace the flight model with Earth-scale dynamics, nor predict interplanetary transfers. Full orbital elements for each planet and encounter prediction remain future work. The local maneuver planner is described below. The overview should not be interpreted as a real-world trajectory planner.

Verdant now uses seeded physical terrain. Flight and map share its mesh and rotation clock; ground clearance measures distance to the actual triangles. Impact markers use the current terrain orientation. Future terrain rotation, thrust and drag remain excluded from the coast preview. The separate Solar System overview remains illustrative; Ochre is a generator preset, not yet a visitable destination. Saved moons are displayed and participate in flight gravity, but the local conic planner does not solve moon encounters. See TERRAIN.md.

## Maneuver planning

During a home-planet coast in space, cut throttle with **X**, open the map with **M**, and click a visible point on the craft's predicted path. A popup offers exactly two actions: **Create plan** and **Warp here**. Clicking elsewhere dismisses it. Creating a plan replaces any previous node and opens six draggable direction symbols at the selected point.

Drag a symbol away from the node to add delta-v in its direction, or toward the node to reduce it. The six handles are **prograde / retrograde**, **normal / anti-normal**, and **radial out / radial in**. Hold **Shift** for one-tenth sensitivity; **Esc** cancels the current drag and restores its original value. The purple preview updates as you drag. Near edge-on views, overlapping handles spread apart so all six remain usable.

The inspector appears after creating a plan. **Precise values** reveals optional signed numeric controls; press Enter to finish typing. **−10 s / +10 s** move the burn time, and **Delete burn** clears the node. Right-drag still rotates the view and scrolling over the map zooms. The editor scrolls separately on smaller windows.

**Warp here** advances the real simulation to the selected predicted time, then returns to **1×** without overshooting. It does not create a plan. Existing altitude, thrust, and debris restrictions still apply; reaching a safety boundary can stop warp before the destination. **X**, **Stop**, manual warp selection, throttle/steering, staging, or editing a plan cancels the automatic warp. Pause holds the target until resumed. Rollout/retry clears it.

Purple shows the planned post-burn trajectory. The panel reports planned apsides, requested and available delta-v, estimated full-throttle duration, fuel required, and a suggested start time that centers the burn on the node. Estimates use currently ignited liquid engines and their remaining feeds. Insufficient fuel or missing engines are explicit; planning never consumes fuel or executes a burn. The navball shows the planned burn direction while the coast prediction is valid.

This is one session-only, instantaneous-burn plan around the home planet. It recalculates while coasting and after staging, clears guidance under thrust, and marks a passed node unavailable. Retrying clears the plan. The approach must remain in vacuum outside the conservative terrain envelope. The purple path stops before atmospheric/terrain entry or significant moon influence; it does not predict encounters. Radial and near-parabolic coasts are unsupported. Long finite burns can differ substantially from the ideal preview. See [implementation scope and tests](FLIGHT_SYSTEM_PLAN.md).
